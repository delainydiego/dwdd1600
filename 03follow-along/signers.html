Who Were The Signers?

Famous Signers

The Declaration of Independence was signed by 56 delegates to the Second Continental Congress. Some of the most famous signers include:

John Hancock
President of the Second Continental Congress.
First (and largest) signature on the document.
His name became synonymous with “signature” in American slang.
Thomas Jefferson
Primary author of the Declaration.
Later became the 3rd U.S. President.
Key advocate of independence and Enlightenment ideals.
Benjamin Franklin
One of the oldest signers (age 70).
Renowned inventor, diplomat, and writer.
Helped secure French support during the Revolution.
John Adams
Strong advocate for independence in Congress.
Later became the 2nd U.S. President.
Played a major role in pushing the Declaration forward.
Samuel Adams
Leader of the Sons of Liberty.
Organizer of resistance like the Boston Tea Party.
Cousin of John Adams.
Richard Henry Lee
Introduced the resolution for independence in Congress.
His motion led directly to drafting the Declaration.
Charles Carroll
The only Catholic signer.
Last surviving signer (died in 1832).
John Witherspoon
Presbyterian minister and educator.
President of Princeton University.
Influential in shaping early American education.

Other notable signers include Benjamin Franklin, Roger Sherman, and Robert R. Livingston. Each of these individuals played a significant role in the founding of the United States and the drafting of the Declaration of Independence.
